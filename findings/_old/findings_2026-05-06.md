## 3) Findings

### 3.1 F₉ scale is irrelevant down to 0.1×

Reducing the F₉ coefficient amplitude from full scale (1.0×) to 10% (0.1×)
with the same noise (σ=0.0007) causes essentially no degradation:
- A-family SR: 99.4% → 98.7% (−0.7 pp)
- B/C/D families: unchanged within noise
- SASCA: flat 50/50 at all 21 rate points across all depths

The attack does not degrade because LDA can still resolve the F₉ structure
even at 0.1× amplitude. The relevant quantity is signal-to-noise ratio for
the F₉ term, not its absolute scale. At σ=0.0007 and F₉ scale=0.1 the SNR
is still high enough for near-perfect template fit.

We did not test 0.05× or below; 0.1× appears to be well above the failure
threshold for this noise level.

### 3.2 Adding HW leakage on top of F₉ does not hurt

HW+F₉ mixed (HW scale=1.0, F₉ scale=1.0) produces A-SR=99.5%, essentially
identical to pure F₉. The HW component is noise relative to the F₉ structure
— it adds variance but not a conflicting signal, so the LDA simply finds F₉.
SASCA remains flat at 100%.

Slight B and C degradation (25.4% → 23.7% and 10.0% → 8.3%) is consistent
with a small noise increase. D actually improves marginally (noise in D's
direction may help regularization, or it is within random variation).

### 3.3 Additive HD is the main template-quality degrader

Adding HD_add (scale=0.5) cuts A-SR roughly in half: 99.4% → 60.3% (pure
HD+F₉), 59.3% (HW+HD+F₉). This is the same HD noise term that produced only
4.1% A-SR on its own (May 5). In the mixed context it introduces a
conflicting signal at the same physical positions — both F₉ and HD leak
at the same clock sample, but with different byte-level functions. LDA partially
decorrelates them but not completely.

Adding HW to HD+F₉ barely changes anything (60.3% → 59.3%), confirming HD
is the bottleneck, not HW.

### 3.4 SASCA is robust to template quality degradation caused by HD mixing

Despite A-SR dropping from 99.4% to ~60% under HD+F₉, SASCA still achieves
50/50 at all 21 rate points, all depths. This is because:
- 60% A-SR means the LLR for the correct byte is still strongly positive
  in the majority of observations; BP converges on enough of them.
- B/C templates are degraded (25.4% → 11.1%, 10.0% → 4.6%) but still
  far above random (0.39%), giving BP enough constraint from all directions.
- The factor graph only needs the overall posterior product to push the
  correct key state above 50% — it tolerates individual template errors well.

The SASCA success threshold is significantly lower than the 0004 template
quality might suggest.

## 4) Implications for the Sim-vs-Real Gap

The May 5 findings established that the remaining gap between v2 F₉ (A=99.4%)
and real silicon (A=35.7%) is a leakage-characterization problem, not a
pipeline problem.

These runs add two data points relevant to that gap:

1. **The real hardware's F₉ signal does not need to be strong.** Even 0.1× F₉
   amplitude (relative to the v2 reference) preserves near-perfect SASCA.
   If the real chip's F₉ coefficients are weak, the simulator can be rescaled
   to match without losing attack viability.

2. **Mixed leakage is recoverable with SASCA.** Even if the real chip leaks
   via a mixture of F₉ and HD (which is plausible — both are related to CMOS
   switching), the SASCA attack still succeeds. Template quality will be
   degraded (A drops to ~60%) but SASCA remains effective.

The 35.7% A-SR on real silicon is consistent with a mixed or noisy F₉ model,
not a fundamentally different leakage physics. The attack path remains viable.
